    What each term means

    Offense categories follow the FBI Uniform Crime Reporting (UCR) definitions that SPD and New York State use for these reports.

    Part I offenses
    The seven categories below, which police agencies nationwide report in a standard way. Arson is also Part I but isn’t included in these totals.
    Violent offenses
    Homicide, rape, robbery and aggravated assault.
    Property offenses
    Burglary, larceny / theft and stolen vehicle.
    Homicide
    Murder and non-negligent manslaughter: the willful killing of one person by another. Excludes accidents, suicides, negligent deaths and justifiable homicides.
    Rape
    Sexual penetration without the victim’s consent, under the FBI definition in use since 2013. Counted in citywide totals but removed from the city’s mapped incident files for privacy.
    Robbery
    Taking or attempting to take something of value from a person by force, threat of force or by putting the person in fear.
    Aggravated assault
    An unlawful attack intended to cause severe bodily injury, usually involving a weapon or other means likely to cause death or serious harm. Assaults without these elements (simple assault) are Part II and not included.
    Burglary
    Unlawful entry into a structure to commit a felony or theft. Force isn’t required.
    Larceny / theft
    Taking property without force, violence or fraud: for example shoplifting, theft from a vehicle, pickpocketing or bicycle theft. Excludes stolen vehicles.
    Stolen vehicle
    Theft or attempted theft of a motor vehicle, such as a car, truck or motorcycle (motor vehicle theft).
    Dispatched calls
    Calls for service that officers were sent to. A call isn’t a crime; many involve no offense at all.
    Year to date (YTD)
    From January 1 through the end date of the latest weekly SPD report.
    Same period last year
    The same weeks of the prior year, so the comparison covers equal time spans.
    5-year average
    The average count for the same weeks across the previous five years, as printed by SPD.
    Preliminary
    SPD’s weekly figures can be revised in later reports as cases are reclassified or reported late.

    Map

    Reported Part I offenses by census tract, from the city’s geocoded incident records.

    Map data not built yet

    The map is built on your computer from public sources. In Terminal, in your contextcrime_live folder, run:

    python3 contextcrime_live.py --build-map --open

    It downloads the city’s 2022–2024 incident records, census tract boundaries and population estimates, which takes about a minute. The daily updates keep the map once it’s built.

    Select a tract

    Tap or hover over an area to see its counts by offense.

    Read this map carefully

    • Counts show where offenses were reported. They don’t measure the probability of victimization for any individual. Tracts with shopping centers, downtown offices, hospitals or the university draw many visitors, which raises counts.
    • Per-resident rates use residents only. Downtown and other low-population tracts produce higher per-resident figures because visitors aren’t counted. Tracts with fewer than 500 residents get no rate.
    • Locations are rounded to the 100 block by SPD for privacy, and rape is excluded from these records, so tract totals won’t match citywide report totals.
    • No rankings. The table lists areas by number, not by count.
    • Council districts are the five areas represented on the Common Council. Their totals include every placed record inside each district.
